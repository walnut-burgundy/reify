# Fox and chicken story fixtures

These are deliberately ordinary-language passages for substitution and transformation examples. The important relationship is not the word `fox` by itself: the fox is a predator in a small world where the corresponding prey is a chicken.

A red fox waited in the weeds beyond the chicken coop. Near dusk, one chicken slipped through a loose board and wandered toward the fence. The fox rushed across the grass, caught the chicken, and ate it beneath the hedge.

---

Three chickens scratched for beetles behind a barn while a fox watched from the edge of the field. The farmer scared the fox away once, but it circled back after dark. By morning one chicken was gone; the fox had eaten it.

---

A fox found a narrow gap under the wire around the henhouse. It squeezed through without waking the dog and crept toward the roost. One chicken fluttered down, the fox caught it, and the fox ate the chicken before escaping through the same gap.

---

The chickens usually stayed close to the shed because a fox had been seen near the woods. During a storm, a gate blew open and one chicken wandered into the orchard. The fox found the chicken there and ate it.

---

At sunrise a farmer counted twelve chickens in the yard. A fox was hiding beside the stone wall. When the flock scattered, the fox chased one chicken into the tall grass, killed it, and ate the chicken.
