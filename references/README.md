## Barry Mazur

### What Is... a Motive?

Barry Mazur, "What Is... a Motive?", *Notices of the American Mathematical
Society* 51(10), 2004, pp. 1214-1216.

- [Official AMS PDF](https://www.ams.org/notices/200410/what-is.pdf)
- [Author's expository-publications page](https://sites.harvard.edu/barry-mazur/expository/)

This article is publicly readable, but no license granting redistribution in
this repository was found. The PDF is therefore linked rather than copied.

### Finding Meaning in Error Terms

Barry Mazur, "Finding Meaning in Error Terms", *Bulletin of the American
Mathematical Society* 45(2), 2008, pp. 185-228.
DOI: [10.1090/S0273-0979-08-01207-X](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-08-01207-X).

- [Harvard DASH record](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/8b7490fa-be83-4c1a-8d20-565e2bc6dafb)
- [Author-hosted preprint](http://abel.math.harvard.edu/~mazur/preprints/meaning_error.pdf)

The DASH record labels its deposited PDF a restricted bitstream and gives the
item terms as "Metadata Only." The author-hosted preprint is publicly readable,
but no explicit redistribution license was found. Neither PDF is copied here.

"Open source" is normally language for software. For a paper, free access alone
does not grant the right to redistribute a copy; an open license or other
permission is needed.

## Predator-prey history and limitations

- Nicolas Bacaër, "Lotka, Volterra and the predator-prey system (1920-1926),"
  in *A Short History of Mathematical Population Dynamics*, 2011.
- Nils Chr. Stenseth et al., "Population regulation in snowshoe hare and
  Canadian lynx: Asymmetric food web configurations between hare and lynx,"
  *PNAS* 94(10), 1997, pp. 5147-5152.
- Bo Deng, "An Inverse Problem: Trappers Drove Hares to Eat Lynx,"
  *Journal of Biological Dynamics* 12(1), 2018, pp. 1-24.

These references are included to prevent the teaching story from hardening into
a claim that catch or fur-return series are direct, passive measurements of a
closed two-species system.
